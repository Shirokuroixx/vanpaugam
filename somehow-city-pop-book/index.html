Somehow, City Pop

A definitive guide to a genre that refused to die,
and the journey across decades it took to become a global phenomenon.

(Launch Date - Late October 2026)

The first comprehensive English-language book on City Pop, told from the perspective of the DJ widely credited with helping revive its popularity in the West. This is both a historical archive and a curated listening experience, built year by year to show how a sound once tied to a specific moment in Japan quietly became a global fascination decades later.

The book unfolds chronologically from 1975 through 1990, with each chapter dedicated to a single year. Instead of treating City Pop as a static genre, it is presented as a living timeline: how it emerged, how it shifted, and how its influence spread through artists, producers, fashion, nightlife, and visual culture. Each year includes detailed artist introductions, chart movements, essential releases, compilation notes, and carefully selected playlists that reflect the sound of that moment as accurately as possible.

Beyond the music, it explores the atmosphere around it. The design trends, the city life, the optimism and economic backdrop, and the visual identity that helped define the era.

The format is intentionally open-ended. You can open the book anywhere and immediately find something useful, whether that is a deep cut recommendation, an artist you have never heard of, or context that reframes a familiar track. It is designed for discovery, not linear reading.

For long-time listeners, it offers structure and depth that has not existed in one place before. For new listeners, it serves as a guided entry point into a catalog that can otherwise feel overwhelming. For collectors and DJs, it functions as a reference built around real listening culture rather than academic distance.

This is a first of its kind release in the West, created for people who already love the sound and for those who are about to discover why it continues to resonate so strongly today.
